    Doomsday Clock

    85 seconds to midnight

    Set January 27, 2026 by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists. It is the closest setting in the clock's history, four seconds nearer than 2025.

    Each step is a year the board moved the hands. The scale is stretched near midnight so recent moves are visible.

    What moved it in 2026

    • Nuclear. Three conflicts involving nuclear-armed states risked escalation during 2025: Russia and Ukraine, India and Pakistan, and the strikes on Iran's nuclear sites. The last US-Russia arms treaty was about to lapse and a return to explosive testing was under discussion.
    • Climate. Carbon dioxide reached 150% of its preindustrial level, and 2024 and 2025 were the warmest years on record.
    • Biology. The board cited research toward "mirror life", AI tools that could help design pathogens, weaker norms against state bioweapons, and reduced US public health capacity.
    • AI and disruptive technology. The US, Russia and China are building AI into their militaries while safety rules were rolled back.

    What the board says would turn it back

    • The US and Russia resuming talks on limiting their arsenals, and every nuclear state keeping the testing moratorium
    • The US, Russia and China agreeing guidelines for AI in military systems, above all nuclear command and control
    • International steps against mirror life and AI-enabled biological threats
    • Rapid cuts in fossil fuel use

    How to read it

    The clock is a judgment by a board of scientists and security experts, set once a year. It is not a measurement or a forecast, and it does not move with daily events. The current setting was made a month before the Iran war began on February 28, 2026, so it does not reflect that war. Past settings have been announced in late January. Read the 2026 statement

    What nearly every list includes

    • Drinking water and food that keeps without a fridge and needs little or no cooking
    • Prescription medicines and a first aid kit
    • A battery or wind-up radio, a torch and spare batteries, and a power bank
    • Cash, and copies of identity and insurance documents
    • Warm clothing and blankets, hygiene supplies, and what infants and pets need

    Germany's civil protection agency suggests starting with three days and building up, and using older stock first so nothing expires.
